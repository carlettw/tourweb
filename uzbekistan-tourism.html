Why Uzbekistan

A living museum of empires

For a thousand years, merchants moving between China and the Mediterranean stopped here — and left behind the finest mosaic domes, madrasas, and minarets in Central Asia. Today the tilework still gleams the way it did when it was fresh, and the streets around it are full and ordinary and alive.

Stop One

Samarkand

Timur's capital, and the city he built to be seen from a distance. The Registan — three madrasas facing one square — is the single most photographed sight in Central Asia, and it earns it.

Stop Two

Bukhara

Smaller and older-feeling than Samarkand, with a whole walkable old city instead of a single monument. Legend says Genghis Khan spared the Kalyan Minaret because his hat fell off looking up at it.

Kalyan Minaret Ark Fortress Lyabi-Hauz Pool Chor Minor
Stop Three

Tashkent

The modern capital — wide Soviet-era boulevards, a metro system famous for stations that look like ballrooms, and a central square that has been renamed at least three times as history moved on.

Independence Square Amir Timur Square Chorsu Bazaar Tashkent Metro
The Classic Route

One line across the map

Most travelers link the three cities by rail — a fast, comfortable way to trace the old caravan road in a few days instead of a few months.
